# Plan: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next decode on the RTX 4080 near Strata's speed (2026-11-05)

**Goal.** Make each decode row cheaper on a 16 GB GPU. Then P3 decodes faster, and MTP drafts pay on CUDA as they do in
Strata. Strata decodes IQ3_S at 85 tokens per second with drafts on the RTX 4080 PC. NanoSeedLM decodes P3 at 41
without drafts ([the Strata probe](../results/2026-11-05-strata-4080-probe.md)).

**Status.** Draft. Matt chose the direction on 2026-11-05: plan the CPU expert path, the cheap exact steps first. A lossy
step becomes the default if its KL divergence passes its gate.

- D1 is on `claude/sleepy-brown-k67z9u`: with a cache, the head's routed experts are its sparse layer `n_layer`.
- D2 on Metal is done and fails gate DB. It is bit for bit, but the expert stage at 3 rows took 9% longer, and decode
  fell 10% (56.9 to 51.5 tokens per second). On Metal the seed matvec waits on each pair's dot products, not on weight
  bytes. Only 29% of pairs share an expert at 3 rows.
- D2 on CUDA fails gate DB by measurement, before a kernel (pc-cuda, `nslm-mova-kbench`). With 21 distinct experts in 30
  pairs, the expert matvec is only 0% to 7% faster than with 30, so L2 does not share them. A seed block is about half
  expansion (shareable) and half dot products for each row. So a perfect kernel saves 13% to 15% at 3 rows, not 25%.
- D3 is on `claude/sleepy-brown-k67z9u` (`4e653a6`). At R = 7 on the RTX 4080, decode is 27% faster (38.2 to 48.6 tokens
  per second) with 32% fewer misses, but gate DL fails. The decode rows' KL against P3's prompt rows rises from 0.035 to
  0.120. So the skip stays off. A sweep of R = 8 and 9, with and without rescaling the kept weights, is next.
- D4's first version (`--cpu-tail R`, off by default) failed gate DC on the RTX 4080 at R = 7. Decode fell 21% (41.3 to
  32.7 tokens per second), and the decode rows' KL rose 13% (0.0351 to 0.0397).
  - The KL rise was a bug. The GPU expert path rounds gate, up, SwiGLU and the down output to BF16. The CPU tail did not.
  - The CPU tail now rounds at the same points. On the tiny model with seed experts, the first decode step then equals
    the GPU's bit for bit. After 20 steps the logits differ by 1.1e-3, against 9.6e-2 with the tail dropped.
  - The speed loss: the GPU waits about 11 ms a token for the CPU, so the CPU work does not overlap the GPU's work.
    `Q4E_PROFILE` now times the CPU tail. Next: pc-cuda measures gate DC again.
- P3H, the MTP head with P = 3 seeds (Matt's question): the CUDA and Metal searches give the same seeds. On the M4 Max,
  code drafts are accepted as often as with the affine head, and chat drafts 4 to 6 points less often. The head takes
  1.26 GB, not 1.57 GB. pc-cuda measures gate DA with P3H on the RTX 4080.

## The gap

A step of T rows costs about 10.5 ms + 7.1 ms x T in Strata, and 9 ms + 25 ms x T in NanoSeedLM. So the cost of a row is
the gap:

| Cost of a row | Strata | NanoSeedLM |
|---|---|---|
| Missed experts a layer | 2.0 to 2.2 | about 3.5 |
| Where a miss goes | The CPU (about 85 µs), some over PCIe | PCIe (about 103 µs), then the GPU |
| Cached experts | GPU time hardly grows with the rows | One matvec pass for each (row, expert) pair |
| MTP head in VRAM | About 0.8 GB | About 1.9 GB, out of the expert cache |

## Steps and gates

| Step | Work | Owner | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | The MTP head's routed experts in the shared expert cache, not fixed in VRAM | cloud | DA |
| D2 | A decode expert matvec by expert: each expert's weights once for all the rows that use it | pc-cuda (CUDA), mac-metal (Metal) | DB |
| D3 | The tail skip: no copy and no compute for a missed expert that every row of the step routes at rank 7 or lower | cloud | DL |
| D4 | The CPU as a second channel for misses, beside PCIe | cloud, pc-cuda | DC |
| D5 | MTP drafts on the RTX 4080 again | pc-cuda | MC of the MTP plan |

- **DA.** The same greedy text. With 1 to 3 drafts, the automatic cache keeps at least 3,100 slots on the RTX 4080
  (2,368 to 2,461 now).
- **DB.** Bit for bit against the current kernel. At 3 rows, the expert stage of a step falls by at least 25%.
- **DL.** On the same text, the KL divergence of the decode rows against the 4-bit reference (`nslm-qwen4-kld --decode`)
  rises by at most 5%. Then the skip is on by default, and an option turns it off.
- **DC.** Decode without drafts is at least 15% faster on the RTX 4080. The decode rows' KL divergence rises by at most
  1%. The CPU sums in another order than the GPU, so the text may change. It must not change from run to run.

## How D4 works

A P = 3 CPU kernel can read the pinned arena as the GPU copies it. Its 16 vector lanes are 16 blocks of one row, and x
is transposed once a call. On one thread it takes one decode layer's 10 experts in 11.5 ms (4.27 G weights per second),
against 12.4 ms for the tiled layout. So each expert lives once in RAM, and either side can compute any miss.

1. The first version sends the tail to the CPU. The tail skip's rule picks the pairs: a missed expert that every row
   routes at rank 7 or later. The CPU computes them, so the result stays exact up to the order of the sums.
2. The GPU writes the picked pairs and the layer input into mapped host memory, and signals (`kq4_signal`). A pool of
   CPU threads computes each picked expert once for all the rows that use it.
3. The GPU copies and computes the other experts meanwhile. `kq4_wait` holds the step until the CPU's outputs are in,
   and a small kernel adds them. The CUDA graph of the decode rows keeps both kernels.
4. At R = 7 the tail skip took 32% of the misses away (27% faster decode). The CPU then computes about 1.1 experts a
   layer and token. That takes about 0.26 ms on 16 threads, beside about 0.5 ms of GPU work a layer.

A split fixed from one text does not carry over. Take the coldest 60% of the experts by one text's routing (the chat,
code, wiki and tide-pools traces). They carry 3% to 14% of that text's routed pairs, but 35% to 75% of another text's. So D4 picks its
pairs from each step's own misses, not from a profile.

The hand-off itself is cheap. On the RTX 4080 under Windows, `nslm-handoff-bench` gives the host's work plus 1.5 to
4 µs. That is about 0.1 to 0.2 ms a forward for 48 hand-offs.

## Costs

- The RTX 4080 PC: free. HF Jobs: the linux CI only, about $0.10 a run.
- D4 needs the CPU seed kernel in the CUDA builds. It needs Clang (`lib_cpu_seed4.c`): the Linux CI and the Windows
  build use GCC and MSVC now. Matt installed LLVM on the PC.

## Risks

- The CPU computes a P3 expert in about 235 µs, against Strata's 85 µs for IQ3_S. So the CPU share is smaller than
  Strata's ([CPU seed rate](../results/2026-11-05-qwen38-4080-cpu-and-streamed.md)).
- The CPU reads and the PCIe copies share the RAM bandwidth (51.2 GB/s peak).
- The tail skip depends on the cache. So its text depends on the VRAM size and the cache state, and not only on the model.
- In a simulation of the tide-pools trace, a CPU share that does not enter the cache raises the misses by about 8%.
